# 2. The Student Financial Dilemma in Emerging Markets

Higher education students in the Philippines operate within an economy heavily dependent on remittances, familial allowances, and gig-economy stipends. The typical weekly student budget fluctuates within the PHP 50 to PHP 5,000 bracket, allocated primarily toward transportation, meals, course materials, and digital utilities. However, structural macroeconomic headwinds severely penalize this demographic.

## 2.1 Purchasing Power Erosion
Over the past decade, the Philippine Peso has experienced structural depreciation against the US Dollar, driven by widening trade deficits, global commodity price shocks, and shifting interest rate differentials set by central banks. When local currency loses value, students holding cash in traditional savings accounts suffer a direct reduction in purchasing power. Textbooks, software subscriptions, and hardware imports scale upward in price, while nominal allowances remain static.

## 2.2 Banking Exclusion & Maintenance Penalties
Traditional commercial banks enforce rigid account maintenance rules. Accounts failing to maintain minimum average daily balances (typically PHP 2,000 to PHP 5,000) incur monthly penalty fees ranging from PHP 300 to PHP 500. For a student managing a weekly budget of PHP 500, these penalties consume up to the entirety of their discretionary savings. Consequently, millions of students remain unbanked or underbanked, relying on physical cash or unoptimized e-wallet balances vulnerable to inflation and impulse spending.
